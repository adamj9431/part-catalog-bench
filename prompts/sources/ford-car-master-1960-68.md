# Using the 1960-68 Ford Car Master Parts Catalog

The catalog contains a text catalog and a separate illustration catalog. Related
functional areas normally use corresponding section numbers.

## Number families

Do not assume that every identifier is the same kind of part number.

### Basic or group numbers

Illustrations usually label components with a basic/group number such as `2455`,
`2B129`, or `13480`. A basic number identifies the kind of component; it is not
necessarily the complete orderable service part number. Use the text catalog,
vehicle year, model, equipment, and description to select a complete applicable
service number when the question asks for one.

Letters can expand a numeric group, such as `2B129`. They are meaningful and
must not be dropped.

### Service part numbers

A typical service number has a four-character prefix, a basic number, and a
design suffix, for example `C7SZ-9092-A`.

For this era, the prefix can communicate the decade/year in which the part was
first introduced, the vehicle line, and the design responsibility. In the
example, `C7` indicates a 1967 introduction and `S` indicates Thunderbird. A
fourth prefix character of `Z` normally identifies a service part number.

The prefix is not sufficient evidence that a part fits the vehicle in the
question. Always use the catalog's applicability listing. A part introduced in
one year can remain in service later, and a service replacement can differ from
the number marked on the original component.

### Engineering part numbers

Engineering numbers resemble service numbers, but the fourth prefix character
is typically a design/engineering-office code rather than `Z`. Do not silently
convert an engineering number into a service number. Report the number family
requested by the question and use the text catalog for the service number.

### Standard and utility hardware

Bolts, nuts, washers, clips, and other standard parts commonly use a five- or
six-digit number followed by an `S` suffix, for example `34445-S`. An optional
number after `S` specifies a finish or variation. `S` alone means plain or
unspecified; examples include `S2` for black finish and `S8` for zinc plating.
The finish code is meaningful: do not remove or change it.

Parenthetical values such as `(M-80)` or `(XX-184)` are dealer locator/bin codes,
not part numbers, unless the question explicitly asks for the locator code.

Hyphens in Ford numbers are inconsistently printed. They may be omitted or
replaced with spaces. Treat `C7SZ-9092-A`, `C7SZ 9092 A`, and `C7SZ9092A` as the
same identifier when their components are otherwise identical. Likewise,
`34445-S8`, `34445 S8`, and `34445S8` are equivalent. Preserve a readable
hyphenated form in your answer when possible.

## Catalog conventions

- Driver's side is the left-hand side of the vehicle.
- `60/` means 1960 and subsequent model years.
- `61/63` means 1961 through 1963, inclusive.
- A single year without a slash applies only to that model year.
- Text listings generally give year, model or equipment, description, quantity
  required per vehicle, and complete part number.
- Standard hardware is often listed immediately below the part with which it is
  used.
- Follow the legend printed on the particular illustration. Symbols can indicate
  kit inclusion, assembly inclusion, non-service status, or a dated change. Do
  not transfer a page-specific symbol meaning to another page without evidence.
- A number next to a leader line identifies the item the line touches, not every
  nearby component.
- Preserve duplicate occurrences when a requested path passes through several
  instances of the same basic number.
